What makes a civilisation?

What makes a civilisation? I wondered this after I finished the Children of Time novel. In the story, humanity essentially destroyed itself at the height of its technological advancement because of a scarily common fact. They had differing world views and were willing to fight to prove that theirs was right.

The difference in the story was that the humans had very good weapons and believed their fight was important enough to use them. What was the result? Everyone suffered when they caused permanent damage to the earth. A few remnants survived and some people left the planet. The rest of the story shows how they became a different species in practice. Humanity, as we knew it, was no more.

This isn’t all that happens in the story. There are many other events like the rise of the spiders, octopuses and some weird planet hive mind thing that are all very fascinating and worth exploring. I’m skipping their full stories not because they’re irrelevant but because I felt they helped show the many routes humans could have taken.

The spiders embraced nature and were one with it. Because of that, they produced similar technology to humans but not at the cost of the planet. They were very disturbed by the way humans did things despite them being a creation of humans.

The octopuses whilst more individualistic still ensured they safeguarded their homes and valued community. Because of this, their creations embraced both their human roots and their unique worldview.

And the planet… Well, I never finished the second book cos it felt like a slog so who knows what happened. It was however an interesting exploration into a society with a completely united worldview and how scary they are.

All this got me asking. What makes a civilisation? Is it our history? Our technology? Our people? Our values? Our stories? I don’t have the answer to these questions and I’m sure there are people smarter than me who do but I ask this to give my reader a moment to reflect.

We already have the means to either elevate ourselves or destroy ourselves. Amazing technology like the Internet has simultaneously connected and divided us. The rise of AI although wildly overhyped is causing both real-world progress and harm.

I watched a video today that gave me pause. It was a video by the YouTuber ThePrimeagen where he read a quote of Open AI’s founder saying they were on the “Wrong side of history” regarding making AI models open source after they suffered mounting pressure from the Deep Seek model that just got released.

I don’t know if they are or are not, but, I did find the phrase “Wrong side of history” fascinating. In that phrase, I find a deep-rooted pride which believes they know what the historians will say. They believe their work is so important it will last for generations and their name will be sung till the end of time.

This might be true but, I’m hesitant to say so. There has never been a lack of great men and women in the vastness of time. Very few were ever written about. Even fewer are still celebrated. Many are sometimes known but few still retain their full messy story. Instead, we get paragons or monsters and try as we might to set the narrative, ultimately it’s the future generations who will label us based on their own ideas despite our best efforts.

So again, I wonder what makes a civilisation? Our history is ever-changing and unreliable. Our technology is ultimately just a tool. Our values are rarely united. And our stories whilst carrying many lessons can often be ignored or actively attacked if they hit too close to home.

I don’t know the answer, but, I do hope whatever it is it’ll continue to exist. For all my high preaching and self-congratulations, I quite like what we have going on this planet. Humanity at its best is awe-inspiring and unbeatable. But at our worst, nothing can protect us from ourselves.
