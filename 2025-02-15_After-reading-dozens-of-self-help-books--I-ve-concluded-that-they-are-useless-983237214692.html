After reading dozens of self-help books, I’ve concluded that they are useless

Self-help books are useless. They won’t change your life, make you a better person, or make you rich. They won’t give you anything.

Why do I say this? It's because it's true. A few pages won’t fix your problems. Can the pages inspire you? Yes, they can. Will that inspiration manifest into tangible results? No, it won’t.

That's the problem with self-help books. It’s in the name. YOU need to HELP YOUSELF and the books won’t do that for you. They can give you a framework. They can emphasize a mindset. Some even provide a step-by-step guide but, in the end, YOU need to do the actual work. YOU need to follow the steps. YOU need to use the framework. YOU need to adopt the mindset.

I’ve read “Think and Grow Rich”, “Rich Dad, Poor Dad”, “The Last Self-Help Book Before Getting Results”, “Soar!”, “Atomic Habits”, “Outwitting the Devil”, “The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People” and so many others. They all said the same thing at their core.

Try. You will get it wrong many times. Keep trying and keep at it. Its hard but if you persist you’ll make it out the other side.

There’s the problem. It's easy to tell someone to keep trying. Its soul-crushing to see failure, after failure, after failure, after failure. Why should you keep trying? You know it's not so bad here. Many people have it way worse. When will you ever get the time to do all these things? You’re always swamped with work. You’re barely paying the bills and here come these people telling you to risk what little safety you have. And for what? For a method you’ve seen doesn’t work. The people who wrote “Think and Grow Rich” and “Rich Dad, Poor Dad” were frauds. Should you be taking your advice from con artists?

There it is. Fear, pretending to be rational. Shame, pretending to be world-wise. Doubt, pretending to be confident. You came to the book looking for an answer. You didn’t like what it said. Why didn’t you like it? It's because it told you what you already knew. It told you the painful truth that you were trying to run away from. So what do you do? You find another book hoping that this time the answer is different. It's not. You search again, and again, and again. Always, the answer is the same. But, something is different. You are getting older. That problem you are “fighting” is getting stronger. Your drive is getting weaker. Eventually, you lose even that small spark of hope you had at the beginning.

You tell yourself the problem was too difficult to solve. Who can defeat that thing? If only you had gotten a lucky break. But, deep down, you hear a voice ask quietly.

“Did you try?”

“Of course I did. Did you not see what I did all these years”

“Did you really try?”

“Can’t you see all the sacrifices I made? Can’t you see I gave everything I had? It’s impossible”

“If you did really try. Why then am I asking you?”

“…”

“You’ve said more now than you ever did”

That’s the danger of self-help books. It's not the fault of the books. You just never came to them for the reasons you thought. When you think about it, you only ever needed to read one of those books. Even the ones written by the crooks have small nuggets of gold. But, you’ll never find them if you don’t go mining.

Self-help books are useless. They won’t change your life, make you a better person, or make you rich. They won’t give you anything. And that's ok.
