I read Animal Farm today

Here’s why I think you should too

I read Animal Farm today. Well, that’s a lie. I listened to a very talented person read Animal Farm today. That’s probably why the story hit me deeper. It’s not a happy story. Maybe it’s a story with some truth. Maybe, it’s all propaganda.

You might be familiar with the story in passing. Maybe you’ve heard its famous quote

“All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.”

You might have some assumptions about the story but, I ask. Have you read it? Have you experienced the story for yourself? I hadn’t before today and I thought I never needed to. If it weren’t for such a well-made audiobook, I probably wouldn’t have experienced the story.

But, I did and I don’t know whether it’s a good or bad thing. The story is very well written. The voice actor went above and beyond to bring it to life. For 3 blissful and painful hours, I was immersed in the goings-on of this simple farm.

You might be familiar with the famous quote

“All animals are equal, but some animals are more equal than others.”

Do you know why it became famous?

I now know, and I now understand.

At first, I empathised with the old pig’s initial speech of freedom. I celebrated the animals' rebellion. The period after the rebellion was a utopian scene. The pigs wrote 7 rules I supported in context.

  1. Whatever walks on two legs is an enemy
  2. Whatever has four legs or wings is a friend
  3. No animal shall wear clothes
  4. No animal shall sleep in a bed
  5. No animal shall drink alcohol
  6. No animal shall kill another animal
  7. All animals are equal

As time passed, I saw those rules slowly distorted, bastardized and finally ignored. It started with small things. Changing not sleeping in all beds to not sleeping in beds with sheets. Changing not drinking alcohol to not drinking alcohol to excess.

That's how it started. Everything fell apart when they changed

No animal shall kill another animal

to

No animal shall kill another animal WITHOUT CAUSE

I never knew the story was so bloody. There are many deaths in the story. First, they were courageous deaths as the animals died for a better future. Then, they were accidental deaths. Then, deaths because of “betrayals”. Then, deaths were used as warnings. And finally, death was used to instil fear and control. In hindsight, it's interesting how useful the phrase “WITHOUT CAUSE” was for the pigs.

All these deaths were horrible but, the most painful death was the death of the loyal worker. There was a horse who fully believed in the pigs. He worked himself to the bone constantly. His friends told him to take it easy but he never listened. He had a drive. He had hope. He was dedicated. And how did the pigs reward him? He was sent to be slaughtered when he was deemed old and useless. His misquoted words were used as justification for the continued oppression of the other animals.

Listening to the story, I wondered. If only those pigs didn’t become so greedy everything would be fine. Would the animals have been better off staying under the farmer’s rule? The sad reality is the pigs did get greedy and the animals were not better off under the farmer.

As the donkey accurately pointed out. Nothing had changed. The animals were never free. The exploitation never stopped. The rebellion was a lie. The animals simply changed one master for another.

There was something I found revealing though. The pigs' greed was indeed one of the reasons that led to the outcome but, it wasn’t the only one. Many times, many many times, the animals sensed there was something wrong. They questioned slightly but never strongly. When they questioned strongly, the pigs weaponized the animals' hatred of humans to pacify them. Many a time the animals mentioned they’d rather be a slave to the pigs than to humans for no other reason than one had four legs and the other had two. They held on to the rules even though they saw them crumbling.

It was then quite ironic that in the end, the pigs used this very distinction to justify their superiority. In the story, rules 1 and 2 were summarized to

Four legs good, two legs bad

Close to the end, when the pigs started walking on two legs, it became

Four legs good, two legs better

I experienced Animal Farm today. I don’t know whether it was a good or bad thing I found it. But, I’m glad I got to experience it if only for the exploration of a powerful idea.
