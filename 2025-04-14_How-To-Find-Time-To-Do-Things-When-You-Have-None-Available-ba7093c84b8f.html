How To Find Time To Do Things When You Have None Available

Have you ever had something you wanted to work on, but you couldn’t find the time?

Maybe it’s because of work. Maybe it’s because of school. Maybe you’ve got responsibilities.

Whatever the reason, the result is the same. That idea, project, activity, etc, never came to be. You never found the time. There’s only so much you can do in one day, after all.

If this problem is something you struggle with, this post might help. Now, I won’t claim this will solve your problem immediately, but it might give you a fighting chance. So I encourage you to read to the end.

A common question I get asked when I talk to people about the things I’m working on is, “Where do you get the time to do all these things?”. Surely, I must have some secret. Maybe I’ve got inhuman willpower or some such.

The true answer is pretty boring. I get the time because I set things up to make sure I have the time.

There are two subjects I always wish are taught to everyone more. They are Philosophy and Psychology. One teaches you how to question deeply, and the other teaches you how you think.

Take these two quotes from “On the Shortness of Life” by Seneca.

It’s not that we have a short time to live, but that we waste much of it. Life is long enough, and it’s been given to us in generous measure for accomplishing the greatest things, if the whole of it is well invested. But when life is squandered through soft and careless living, and when it’s spent on no worthwhile pursuit, death finally presses and we realize that the life which we didn’t notice passing has passed away.
…
Men are thrifty in guarding their private property, but as soon as it comes to wasting time, they are most extravagant with the one commodity for which it’s respectable to be greedy.

Whilst powerful quotes, they can also be misinterpreted to mean that you should nickel and dim every moment of your life and live like a robot that’s not having fun. That’s not correct. Seneca is simply asking if you are aware of how you spend your time. Have you ever taken a moment to reflect on what you spend your time on on a daily, weekly, or monthly basis?

The sad reality is that many people don’t. It’s not their fault, there are too many things that can distract us these days. But the consequences of our inattentiveness are ours alone to bear.

Here is an interesting experiment you can carry out. Find someone you know who regularly uses TikTok and spend some time observing them while they use it. You’ll notice very quickly that seconds, minutes, or even hours can pass by with them glued to the screen. The strange thing is, though, that when you ask them how they felt about the time they spent on the app, most of the time, they’re likely to be indifferent. Either they didn’t notice the time passing or passing the time was their goal in the first place.

This is what it means to not be greedy with your time. I might have used TikTok as an example, but you can replace this with anything. Be it YouTube, Instagram, Netflix, video games, excessive partying, etc. None of these things are bad, but if you aren’t careful, you will spend way too much time than is healthy.

We all only have 24 hours in a day. If we remove 8 hours for work/school, 8 hours for sleep, and maybe 4 hours for miscellaneous stuff, that only leaves us with 4 hours every day. I hope you see you don’t have a lot of time to yourself. It’s really important you use this time for things you’ll actually enjoy and find fulfilling rather than things that just pass the time.

So yeah, how do I find the time? I looked at what I spent my free time on, tried to find somewhere I could squeeze in at least 1 to 2 hours, and just tried to do that every day.

That’s all there is to it. You’d be surprised how much you can get done by working on something for an hour or two every day or most days.
