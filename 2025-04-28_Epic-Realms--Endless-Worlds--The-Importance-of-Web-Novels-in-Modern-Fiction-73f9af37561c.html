Epic Realms, Endless Worlds: The Importance of Web Novels in Modern Fiction

Because who needs ‘the end’ when you can have Chapter 999+

In my opening article, I mentioned that we’re all about web novels here at “Chapter 999+”. But that begs the question, why? Anyone reasonably in the know should realise that web novels are not an endangered species to be protected and taken care of. It's a sprawling community with thousands, if not millions, of readers and creators worldwide who actively enjoy all the various unique stories.

The reason is quite simple. Having spent years immersed in reading web novels, I’ve come to notice that, as a whole, the authors are not taken seriously. It's not uncommon to hear that a prolific webnovel author only gets acclaim once their work is adapted to a light novel, if it's in Japan or a Kindle exclusive if it's in the West. The implied meaning is that they’ve only “made it” once their work goes beyond the very platforms that made them famous in the first place.

Let’s use the recently popular anime Solo Levelling as an example. Many people are aware that the anime is based on the Korean Manhwa of the same name, but not as many people are aware of the web novel that started the story. It's the same thing with Reincarnated as a Slime, Overlord, Rising of the Shield Hero, Mushoku Tensei, and so many others.

Once the author's work becomes popular and is published somewhere like Kindle or as a light novel, the original webnovel is no longer mentioned. The published work is now treated as the “original” source, and all other subsequent media stem from that. In certain cases, like when the work joins the Kindle Unlimited program, the author is legally required to take down their work from all other platforms except Kindle, which leads to a great loss of the history of the work.

I understand why the authors do this. After all, they worked so hard to get to where they are, and deserve the financial compensation like any other mainstream author. It's just that I always feel sad when I see that such a key part of their history is overlooked and downplayed by people not in the webnovel sphere. Many of the most popular manga/manhua/manhwa and anime would not have existed were it not for the vibrant webnovel community. Many times, they’re the source from which many ideas stemmed, cross-pollinated and eventually got refined from.

This is why I feel so upset when I see web novel authors simply thrown under the lump of “amateur writers”. People who are still “figuring it out” and need to “refine their work”. This ironically ignores the fact that when their work is eventually ported to an official publication, the works rarely change substantially. This, on its own, proves how talented the authors were in the first place.

It's honestly quite frustrating seeing this cycle repeat itself again and again. The worst part of it is that some popular platforms are aware of this asymmetry and use it as a means to exploit talented authors who haven’t been recognised yet. Take, for instance, the situation with Kindle if the author chooses to put their works on Kindle Unlimited. Although they are promised better reach and revenue shares, they also have their distribution heavily limited. They can only publish to Kindle Unlimited, and their revenue is based on how many pages are read a month. This is honestly very exploitative and unsustainable because it forces the author to somehow get a continuous supply of eyeballs or drag stories beyond recognition. It's honestly a losing battle in the long run as it's pretty rare for a person to reread the same story, and if a story seems like it doesn’t end, it will eventually experience a drop off in readership. This is also exacerbated by the nature of Kindle Unlimited. Honestly, there’s a lot of works and at any moment, readers can get distracted and jump to a different story.

I used Kindle as an example, but they’re not the only perpetrator. Another known bad-faith actor is Webnovel. They are also known for their very restrictive and unfair contract and revenue-sharing structure.

This is what hurts me so much about web novel authors. Their works are not treated with the respect they deserve, and because of this, they end up signing very unfavourable contracts because they aren’t aware of the true value of their work.

This needs to end, and I think the first step is allowing these authors to understand they are valued. Some of the most popular anime and manga came from those humble web novel authors. They deserve respect and appreciation. These are talented individuals who are expanding on what literature could be. Their worlds can be as deep as that created by Tolkien. Their politics are as gripping as in Game of Thrones. And, they can give you absurdities as in the Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy.

So I hope you realise that web novels do matter, and that's why we at Chapter 999+ will keep talking about them.
