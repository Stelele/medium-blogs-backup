A Bard, a Goblin, and an Innkeeper Walk Into a World…

Where Every Guest Has a Story, and Every Story Leaves a Scar

Picture this scene. You were at home enjoying your simple life. Its nothing crazy just average, simple, tame. Suddenly, without any warning, or consent from you, you’re wisked away to an open plain with grass going all the way to the horizon.

MMM, maybe you had one too many drinks that you weren’t supposed to. So you start looking around to see if you can figure out where you are. After a few misadventures, you eventually discover that not only are you not in the same city as your now previous home, you’re not even in the same universe.

The wisked away to a different world isn’t an uncommon trope in media. Its pretty popular in Japanese media giving it the genre of Isekai. Aside from that, many stories in the West like Wizard of Oz and Alice in Wonderland also share similar elements. Its a nice trope and offers the reader a chance to really dig deep into a new alien world and understand it bit by bit.

Sadly, we’ve gone waaay past the era where simply going to an alien world is interesting. No one cares if you drew a fancy map and came up with a unique magic system powered by the power of breathing if there’s nothing interesting happening in said world. I’ll also go on the record that I generally hate stories that let the main protagonist start of strong from the begining. I have no problems with them being super OP in time, but I like accompanying them to that journey of mastery.

This is what makes it so hard for me when it comes to isekai stories. Many a time, the characters end up feeling as if they only exist as a means for me, the reader, to insert myself in the story. Whilst that strategy may work for short stories, you can’t tell epic tales with such an approach and here at Chapter 999+, we’re all about epic long winded tales.

So, you might wonder, where is this mythical isekai story you are describing. Ladies and gentelman, I present to you ‘The Wandering Inn’. Now I admit I cheated a bit since its not a story from Japan but I’ve been in the webnovel business long enough to know that these guys are aways cross polinating ideas.

At first glance, you might think that Erin the main protagonist of the story is just a generic character that gets sent to another world and uses the power to plot armor to become ridiculously strong. Weeelllll, she does have a ton of plot armor and is increasingly becoming scary strong but trust me here, she’s far from generic.

Normaly when you hear that a character is complex, you associate them with being some form of anti hero that’s just misunderstood. Before I read the Wandering Inn, I can’t say that stereotype was wrong. It’s such a breath of fresh air to meet such layered characters. They all have great depth and are each struggling with their own inner demons. Even the demons and monsters are struggling with their own issues and identity.

Its hard for me to express what makes this story so great without spoiling it but trust me, its wonderful. Everything from the world building to the characters to the various plots going on is just impressive and a sight to behold. You can read the story completly for free on her website. If you’d rather experience it as an audiobook its also up on audible.

Check it out. Enjoy yourself. And remember to not leave your frying pan for safety.
