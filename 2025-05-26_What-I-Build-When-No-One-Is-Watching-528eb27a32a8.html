When no one is watching, I build weird, half-finished, and often janky software projects. I love them — all of them.

They don’t scale. They don’t win hackathons. Some crash if you breathe on them too hard. But still — they’re mine. They’re the products of late-night curiosity and quiet determination. They’re the closest I’ve ever come to being the inventor I once told my teacher I wanted to be.

She laughed, by the way.

Not in a cruel way — just like I had said something naive. “Be serious,” she said. “What’s an inventor?”
 To be fair, I didn’t know either. I still don’t. But I never forgot that moment.

✨ The Side Quest of Creation

Most of my projects start with a spark:

“What if I built this thing?”

Sometimes it’s a problem I care about. Sometimes it’s just a vibe. And sometimes — let’s be honest — it’s just to avoid dealing with other parts of life. I open the terminal like it’s a portal. And for a while, I get to be a little god in a tiny world that I can shape and debug as I please.

But here’s the catch: I rarely finish them.

Usually, I lose interest the moment I figure out how it works. The mystery evaporates. The quest completes itself in my head. And that’s often enough.

“You’ve unlocked the blueprint. The magic fades. Side quest abandoned.”

It’s funny, isn’t it? I chase the unknown… but the moment it becomes familiar, I start to slip away.

🧠 A Brain That Doesn’t Always Play Fair

There’s a pattern here, and I know it well. Sometimes it’s boredom. Sometimes it’s fear disguised as logic.
 My mind is excellent at crafting convincing reasons to stop:

“This won’t scale.”
 “Someone else already built it better.”
 “You’ve learned what you needed.”
 “No one will ever use this anyway.”

All of those are technically true. None of them will be the real reason.

The truth is, I struggle with the space between potential and completion. In that space, I either lose interest… or I lose nerve. And I’ve become very skilled at telling stories that make both feel justified.

⚙️ My Beautiful Broken Builds

I’ve made:

  • Programs to play around with colours
  • Text-based games with no endings
  • A voice-reading web app called Cloud Reader that’s weirdly functional and proudly incomplete
  • More idea.txt files than I can count

And every single one taught me something. About code. About systems. About myself.

They also taught me that it’s okay to build badly. That perfection is optional. That shipping something ugly is still better than leaving it in my head.

🛠 Why I Keep Building Anyway

Because I still want to be an inventor.
 Even if no one calls me one.
 Even if I never finish “the big one.”

I build because I need to. Because without creation, I start to unravel. Coding gave me a way to turn my thoughts into something tangible. Because even janky websites feel like home.

And because even if these little side quests never go anywhere…
 They remind me that I’m still moving forward.

So, what do I build when no one is watching?

I build whatever I want.
 I build because I can.
 And I build because some part of me still believes — deep down — that ideas can shape the world.

Thanks for walking with me.
 Until next time
