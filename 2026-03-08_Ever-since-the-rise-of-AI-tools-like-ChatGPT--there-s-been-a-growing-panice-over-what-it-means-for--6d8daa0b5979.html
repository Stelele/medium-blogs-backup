Ever since the rise of AI tools like ChatGPT, there’s been a growing panice over what it means for numerous professions. “AI will take your job” has been a running theme from many techno-optimists whilst for the techno-persimists, they claim that will never happen. To further add mud in the water, we’re seeing a growing number of companies firing large numbers of their staff claiming they don’t need them now that AI has come. Its pretty interesting to note that a lot of them end up walking back those decisions silently or loudly too but some companies don’t.
To speak on my profession, “vibe coding” has become a theme with people claiming that they can make entire apps with just prompts. This is usually pretty unrealistic but, if you ignore the shodiness of the whole affair, you have to admit its amazing the thing even runs in the first place. Something that does seem more realistic are tools like cursor or Copilot that help with certain aspects of coding which is usually around boilerplate generic code statements. Aside from this, other people claim programmers should now become project managers that should orchastrate and manage the multiple AI agents doing the actual coding. As you can see, there’s a lot of claims being made but lets put that on the back burner and move to a different field.
A particularly sensitive area for many people regarding AI is in art. Not long ago, the o4 model enjoyed a viral moment when it was found that it could make images in the style of Studio Ghibli movies. This prompted me to experiment with it on my own and I’ve been using it for my thumbnails ever since. It felt so nice to see an image of what I envisioned as a person who isn’t good in both physical and digital art. Aside from that, we are seeing a growing number of AI written articles. This again has caused a lot of polarization with questions on whether they should be banned entirely or not. I’ve played around in this space too and whilst I won’t claim to be a good writter, I understand the appeal of wanting to use these tools.
I constantly struggle with writter’s block whenever I try and write an article. I often find myself sitting on my laptop with ideas floating through my mind but no words landing on my blank page. I realize every one will mention that I should just power through it and start with something. I also know that if you’ve written something, you know that’s easier said than done. Its in those moments of weakness that AI tools feel so appealing. The thought of just telling it what you want and it giving you an article is pretty awesome. Its extra interesting if you somehow manage to force yourself to make a draft first and then produce multiple variations of it in diffrent styles and voices. Oh you don’t want to bother with grammer and ask it to fix the document in one shot. Its fast, its convinient but, its not without its own cost too. You loose the many benefits that writing gives you like room for reflection and refinement of ideas. In focusing on just getting the thing, you sometimes forget that the journey to the thing is also important.
Now I’m not above saying this is always the case. Sometimes its really just about the result and nothing more. Personally, I don’t have nearly as much resistance to using AI when it comes to this class of tasks. The difficulty with this though is the perception. I see getting an image for my blog post as a means to an end. I want an image that best reflects the contents of my article without infrignig on any copyright. I’d very much like it if it won’t cost me a lot. Hence, I use ChatGPT to make me an image that fits this criteria and I love it. The problem though is my present day convenience is built about the very real exploitation of numerous artists work that was used to train said model. AI art at present if fundamentally derivative. Its great at imitating other people’s styles but not so much coming up with its own. For me, I don’t mind as many of my images purposly use that similar style to enhance my point. So … how do I square this circle? How do I justify my present benefit if its negatively affecting someone else? I don’t have the answer for this dilema at the moment and its why for instance, I understand Mediums verdict that you need to disclose when and where you use AI so that the other person can decide how they interact with the content.
So … whats the takeaway of this long ramble I’ve been going on. Am I saying you should stop using AI tools? No, they are here to stay and they have their uses. Am I saying you should dive head first into the hype? No, thats stupid and you’ll hurt yourself. Maybe the right answer lies somewhere in the middle. You use it enough to enrich your life, but you don’t become a slave to it. Thats a pretty vague line though. Its made even vaguer by the fact that AI is perceived to be the most usefull for things you’re not an expert in. I can spot all the flaws and mistakes if I use ChatGPT or Cursor to write code for me. I also think ChatGPT is a divine artist whenever I use it to make an image for me. I’m unsure of its compentancies when it comes to writing articles on its own, but I do see it as a helpful editor. In reality, ChatGPT is mostly average in any of the areas I highlighted. I just perceive it as being more or less skilled based on my own knowledge and skills.
I think that should be the important thing to remember when you use these tools. They are the most helpful when you have deep knowledge and you just need a bit of help. They’re the least helpful when you have no knowledge and treat them as your bible. They’re sorta usefull if you’re somewhere in the middle.
